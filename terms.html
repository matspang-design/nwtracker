The short version

NWtracker is a private program one individual wrote for their own household. It is free, it is offered to nobody else, and it comes with no warranty of any kind. It reads financial data in order to show it back to you; it never moves money.

1What this is

NWtracker is a personal budgeting and net-worth program. It reads a household's own financial records — bank exports, broker statements, and where connected, a bank account or crypto exchange over a read-only interface — and presents them as balances, spending and net worth on a dashboard served on the operator's own computer.

2Who may use it

The private individual who operates it, and members of their household whom that person adds. It is not a product, not a service, and not offered to the public. There is no sign-up, no fee and no support.

3No fee, and no commercial use

Nothing is charged, because nothing is sold. The program is not used for any business or professional purpose, and no third party's data is processed through it.

4It is not a financial service

NWtracker holds no money, moves no money and executes no orders. It does not give financial, tax, investment or legal advice, and nothing it displays should be treated as any of those. Figures shown are a reading of the records it was given; decisions taken on them are the operator's own.

5Read-only by design

Where a bank is connected, the access requested covers balances and transactions only, and the payment-initiation interfaces are never called. Where an exchange is connected, the API key is expected to carry read permission alone. The operator is responsible for creating keys with the narrowest permissions that work.

6Credentials are the operator's responsibility

API keys, their secrets, and the private key used to sign open-banking requests live on the operator's computer. Keeping that computer secure, and retiring a credential that may have been exposed, is the operator's job. The program never transmits a credential to anyone but the institution it authenticates against.

7Accuracy

The program checks its own arithmetic where it can — comparing imported figures against the totals the source documents state themselves — and reports what does not reconcile rather than hiding it. It is nonetheless software written by one person, and it may be wrong. The authoritative record of any account is the statement issued by the institution that holds it, not this program.
